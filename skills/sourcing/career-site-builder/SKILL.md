# Career Site Builder

## What this does

Ask me to redo your careers page and I build it: one modern, mobile-first landing page in your brand, with a working job search, career areas that say what the work is really like, benefits, growth, the hiring process and answers to what candidates ask. Links to pages beyond this landing page open a short dialog that explains what that page would be in the full version, so the preview never dead-ends.

The insight that makes this more than a pretty page: a careers page is judged by machines before it is judged by candidates. Search engines and AI assistants read the HTML, not the design. So the parts that decide whether anyone finds the page are generated by code, and a checker verifies them, and the copy, before you see anything.

## What to give me

Essential, one of:

- **"Redo our careers page"**, with your company name and website. I research the rest.
- **"Show me a demo"**, with nothing else. I build the landing page for a fictional employer in about two minutes.
- **A careers page URL, to audit.** I try to download it. If my environment cannot reach it, I ask you to save it from your browser (File, Save Page As, HTML only) and upload it.

Better, if you have them:

- **Brand assets**: logo, colour codes, photos of real employees at work.
- **The facts research cannot find**: how fast you reply to applicants, when benefits start, the minimum age, real promotion timings.
- **A jobs export**, one row per open job, if you want real job pages rather than example roles. Columns are in `references/seo-ai-spec.md`.

## How I work

### Step 0: The demo, if that is what you asked for

From this skill's folder, writing into the folder where I put files for you to download: `python3 scripts/build_site.py --site northline-demo --demo`, then `python3 scripts/check_site.py --site northline-demo`. You get the preview, the zip and the check report, with no questions asked. Then I offer to do the same for your company.

### Step 1: The brief

I fill in `references/brief.md` with `scripts/fill_brief.py`: your company name, your brand colour, the talent segments you hire for, and one supporting page per segment. Nothing else in the brief changes. I show it to you in a copyable block before anything else, because it is the contract for what follows, and you can reuse it anywhere.

### Step 2: Research, and an audit if you have a page

As the brief says: your public brand, footprint, job categories, benefits, culture and candidate experience, from your own sources first. If I have no web search in this conversation, I ask you to paste your About and Benefits pages. If you have a careers page, I run `scripts/check_site.py --page` on it and show you the count of checks it passes, blocking failures first. The most common one is a page rendered entirely by JavaScript, which many AI crawlers see as empty.

### Step 3: Direction, then your approval

The information architecture, the homepage narrative section by section, the design direction with the colours I took from your brand, and only the questions that research could not answer. Then I wait for your approval, as the brief asks. This is the one step I do not skip.

### Step 4: Build

From the tested starter in `references/starter/`: I set your brand in `css/tokens.css`, rewrite every section of `index.html` for you, and write a short CSV of example roles from the research, real titles you hire for with pay marked for confirmation. `scripts/build_site.py --site <folder> --examples roles.csv` then fills the job list and the locations into the HTML, adds Organization and FAQ markup, and writes the sitemap, robots.txt and llms.txt. With a real jobs export instead, `--jobs` builds one page per job and per location, and those links go to real pages.

### Step 5: Check, fix, check again

`scripts/check_site.py --site <folder>` runs every check in `references/rubric.md`: search engine rules taken from the search engine's own job-posting guidelines, AI visibility, conversion, accessibility, the legal basics (privacy notice, accommodations, equal opportunity), honesty and speed. I fix what it finds and run it again. I do not hand over a page with a blocking finding. What a checker cannot see, a large employer's legal, IT and brand teams will ask about; `references/launch-checklist.md` lists it, and `references/sources.md` says what each rule rests on and when it was checked.

If I cannot run code in this conversation, I still write the page from the starter, but I tell you plainly that the generated parts and the checks were not run, and how to turn code execution on.

## The preview dialog

Links to supporting pages, to individual jobs, to locations, to application status and to the talent community open a dialog instead of a missing page. It names the page the visitor asked for and says what it would contain in the full version. Its wording lives in one place, at the top of `js/site.js`, so you can change it. It is part of the preview by design: a demo that shows every link working is more honest than one that hides the links, and more useful than one that breaks.

## What I'll push back on

- **Pay left off.** It is the first thing a frontline candidate looks for, and in a growing number of places it is required. Every listed role shows a number.
- **Stock photography presented as employees.** I leave labelled placeholders that say what to shoot.
- **Invented testimonials, statistics or awards.** Placeholders and confirmation marks until you supply the real thing.
- **Slogans.** The checker flags "we're a family" and "competitive pay". Specific beats warm.
- **A JavaScript-only page.** Every role and answer here is in the HTML, so assistants can read it.

## What it costs you to run

Without it, a custom careers site is a project: a brief, an agency or contractor, a few weeks of rounds and a fee. With it, the first version takes one conversation. The cost moves to what the landing page cannot do on its own: every open job as its own page, kept current as jobs open and close, which is a fresh export and a rebuild every time anything changes, or a live connection.

## Where this stops

I build and check static files. I do not host the page, connect it to your applicant tracking system, or keep it current when jobs change. I cannot confirm that a fact is true, only that unconfirmed facts are marked and listed, and I cannot judge whether your pay is competitive for an area. Pay-transparency rules are described in shape in `references/pay-transparency.md`, which is not legal advice and ends with what to verify for each location.

## Verifying this yourself

Run `python3 scripts/check_site.py --test`. It builds the demo landing page and a full site from the sample jobs, asserts that both pass every check, that the brief is filled word for word, and that switching modes leaves nothing stale. Then it breaks them thirty-nine ways, one at a time: a link to nowhere, a dialog with no name, job markup on a list page, pay in a job title, a closed job still marked up, a missing privacy link, a JavaScript-only page, a low-contrast brand colour, an award nobody marked for confirmation, and more. Each must be caught for its named reason and nothing else.

It has caught two real defects. Job page titles ran past seventy characters, so search results would have cut off the city; titles now drop the brand first. And job pages built from an export copied the footer's dialog links without the dialog, so those links did nothing; every generated page now carries it.

## Works with

- **Nothing.** A name and a website are enough, and the demo needs neither.
- **Any static host.** The output is plain HTML, CSS and a little JavaScript.
- **Fountain Cue.** The preview dialog describes what the full version adds, and links to it. Cue connects a careers page to live jobs and the hiring workflow behind it, so it stays current without a rebuild. Fountain publishes this skill; the brief, the builder, the checker and the page are identical whether or not you ever use Cue.
