# From curious visitor to applicant

Frontline candidates compare several jobs at once, on a phone, often in a few spare
minutes. Each rule here removes a reason to leave.

## On every page

- **Search jobs in the header**, visible without opening a menu on a phone.
- **Location first.** Distance and commute decide more frontline applications than
  anything else on the page. City or ZIP is a first-class search field, and every
  location has its own page.
- **No dead ends.** Every page ends with a next step: a job search, a filtered list,
  or the talent community.

## On every job page

- **Pay as a number**, above the fold. "Competitive" is read as "low".
- **The schedule in one sentence**: start times, weekends, how far ahead it is posted.
- **Apply in one tap**, with how long it takes.
- **Announce the hand-off.** When Apply leads to an applicant tracking system that looks
  different, say so next to the button. An unexplained change of look is where many
  candidates decide the link is a scam and leave.
- **Requirements that are real.** Each extra requirement filters people out. Licence,
  age and physical demands stay; years of experience for entry-level work go.
- **A realistic preview.** If the work is cold, physical or repetitive, the page says
  so. It costs a few applications and saves early quits.

## On the home page

- One concrete promise in the hero, not a slogan. "Hear back within 48 hours" beats
  "Join our family".
- A path for people who are still deciding: what each kind of job is like, side by side.
- The hiring process with timings, because "how long until I get paid" is the question
  behind most frontline applications.
- A talent community for people whose job is not open yet, with what they will
  receive and how to stop it.

## Languages

If a meaningful share of the workforce prefers another language, offer the key pages
in it: home, the main career area, job pages, FAQ, hiring process. A translated home
page that leads to English-only job pages loses the candidate at the moment that matters.

## What I will push back on

- **Hiding pay** "until the interview". It is the first thing candidates look for, and
  in a growing number of places it is required. See `pay-transparency.md`.
- **An account before applying.** Every extra step before the first question loses
  candidates, and a password is the most expensive step of all.
- **A video or animation as the hero.** It costs seconds on a phone connection, and the
  candidate came for the search box.
- **Stock photos of smiling people presented as staff.** Candidates recognise them, and
  it undoes the specific, honest copy around them.
