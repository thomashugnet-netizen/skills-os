# Talent segments by kind of employer

A starting point, not a template. The research decides which segments an employer
really hires for; this file decides what each segment's page has to answer.

## What every segment page answers

1. What the work involves, hour by hour, including the hard parts
2. The schedule shape: start times, weekends, how far ahead it is posted
3. Starting pay, as a number, and what moves it
4. Requirements that are real (licence, age, lifting) and nothing aspirational
5. The next step up and how long it usually takes
6. A link to that segment's open jobs, filtered

## Retail and multi-site stores

| Segment | Page name | What candidates most want to know |
|---|---|---|
| Store team members | Store teams | Shift times, weekend expectations, whether experience is needed |
| Shift leads and store managers | Store leadership | Pay step from team member, bonus, how many hours a manager really works |
| Stocking and receiving | Often folded into store teams | Early or overnight starts, physical load |
| District and regional roles | Store leadership | Travel, number of stores, who it reports to |
| Support centre | Corporate | Hybrid or on site, store visits |

## Quick service restaurants

| Segment | Page name | What candidates most want to know |
|---|---|---|
| Crew | Restaurant crew | Shift length, meal policy, school-friendly hours |
| Shift and restaurant managers | Restaurant leadership | Closing duties, bonus, path to general manager |
| Delivery and drive-thru specialists | Crew, unless large enough for their own page | Tips, vehicle, hours |

QSR candidates skew younger and apply fastest of any segment. The minimum age and
work-permit rules for minors belong in the FAQ, marked for confirmation.

## Logistics and delivery (employed drivers and warehouse)

| Segment | Page name | What candidates most want to know |
|---|---|---|
| Warehouse associates and selectors | Warehouse | Fixed shift or rotating, overtime, how heavy the work is |
| Drivers | Driving | Licence class, home time, routes, equipment |
| Technicians | Maintenance and trades | On call, vehicle and tools, certifications paid for |
| Operations leads | Operations leadership | Span of control, shift pattern |

Safety screening before day one (drug screens, medical cards) is a real step and
belongs in the hiring process page, described in shape rather than in detail.

## Gig and on-demand delivery

There is no interview and no offer: sign up, verify, get activated, complete a first
job. The site is shorter and the page that matters most is "How to get started", with
the verification steps and how long activation takes. Pay is explained as how earnings
work, not as a wage, and any example earnings must be marked for confirmation.

## Corporate, in any of the above

One page is usually enough. It should say plainly that most corporate staff work
closely with the front line, if that is true, because it is what separates this
employer's office jobs from everyone else's.
